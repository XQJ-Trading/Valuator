**The US-Iran conflict, known as the 2026 Iran War or Operation Epic Fury, began on February 28, 2026, with US and Israeli airstrikes on Iranian military sites, leadership, nuclear-related targets, and infrastructure, prompting Iranian missile and drone retaliation against US bases, Israel, and regional allies.** As of early March 2026, major combat zones include Iran, Israel, and US bases in Bahrain, UAE, Kuwait, Jordan, and Saudi Arabia, with strikes disrupting oil shipments in the Strait of Hormuz.[1][2][3][4]

### Major Combat Zones and Strike Targets
- **Iran**: Primary focus of US-Israeli strikes, including missiles, air defenses, military infrastructure, missile launchers in western Iran, Basij headquarters, and leadership sites. Nearly 900 strikes occurred in the first 12 hours on February 28, targeting nuclear capabilities, missile programs, and naval forces to prevent retaliation and topple the regime.[1][2][4]
- **Nuclear facilities**: US objectives explicitly include preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons, with strikes launched amid active nuclear negotiations.[2][3]
- **Oil refineries and infrastructure**: Iranian retaliation targeted oil infrastructure, including vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, causing worldwide oil shipment disruptions. No confirmed US strikes on Iranian refineries in initial reports.[3][4]
- **US bases and allies**: Iran struck 14 US bases in Bahrain, UAE, Kuwait, Jordan, and Saudi Arabia, plus Israel and US embassies.[2]
- **Other**: Israeli jets hit cities and sites across Iran; strikes also affected major air hubs.[1][3]

Combat intensified in the first week (February 28–March 6), with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio announcing increased strike intensity on March 4.[1]

### Casualty and Damage Reports (February–March 2026)
- **Iran**: Over 2,000 dead from initial strikes, including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and dozens of officials. A missile hit a girls’ school near a naval base in Minab (Bandar Abbas), killing about 170 civilians. Destruction at air hubs, civilian areas, and military sites; Mojtaba Khamenei survived an airstrike.[1][3][4]
- **Regional**: Hundreds dead in Lebanon and Israel from Iranian retaliation; hundreds of thousands of travelers stranded due to air disruptions.[4]
- **Iranian response**: Missile and drone barrages across the Middle East, with IRGC confirming ongoing strikes until US-Israel "defeat."[2]
No comprehensive March casualty tallies available in reports up to March 4; Iranian transitional council formed post-Khamenei.[3]

Strikes coincided with Ramadan and followed US naval deployments (e.g., USS Gerald R. Ford) and protests in Iran.[1][3] US goals emphasize regime destabilization and protecting interests from Iran's Axis of Resistance.[2]
